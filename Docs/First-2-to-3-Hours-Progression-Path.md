# First 2 to 3 Hours Progression Path

- Project: Witch's Pantry
- Engine: Unity 6; current project version: `Witch-Pantry/ProjectSettings/ProjectVersion.txt`
- Purpose: Turn the current unlock and upgrade model into a concrete early-game progression path for the first 2 to 3 hours of play.

## Summary

The first 2 to 3 hours should teach:

- machine placement
- visible production flow
- simple adjacency
- customer demand
- the first branch beyond Healing Potion
- the first meaningful room-pressure moment
- the first prestige approach, but not necessarily a full prestige in the first 2 hours

The player should feel:

- smart quickly
- productive visibly
- guided by demand
- pulled toward better layouts
- excited by the next branch instead of buried under systems

## Core Early-Game Rule

The player should almost always have one clear next goal from this list:

- buy a machine
- unlock a branch
- fix a bottleneck
- improve a room layout
- satisfy a featured contract
- prepare for the next room or prestige milestone

If the game instead says:

`Please enjoy 14 equal priorities and a vague sense of destiny`

the progression is failing.

## Early-Game Progression Shape

Use this rough structure:

### 0 to 10 Minutes

Teach:

- the pantry is spatial
- machines create a visible chain
- the player can improve flow with placement

Unlock and goal sequence:

1. Start with:
   - Herb Garden
   - Well
   - one small pantry room
2. Introduce:
   - Enchanted Cauldron
   - one simple customer or demand prompt
3. Fast first purchase goal:
   - buy or place Mortar Golem
4. First full loop:
   - Herb
   - Water
   - Ground Herb
   - Healing Potion

Expected player moments:

- `I made the room actually do something`
- `I can see why this machine is waiting`
- `moving these closer makes more sense`

What should unlock during this phase:

- basic machine selection UI
- first machine upgrade
- first featured demand card

What should not unlock yet:

- second room
- research tree
- complex faction layers

## 10 to 30 Minutes

Teach:

- bottlenecks are readable
- upgrades matter
- demand gives direction
- there is more than one potion path

Recommended sequence:

1. The player buys:
   - one or two more Herb Gardens or Wells
   - first machine upgrades for core throughput
2. The player sees:
   - a stable Healing Potion loop
   - first contract streak or featured order
3. Unlock:
   - Mushroom Cave
   - Mushroom branch visibility
4. Introduce:
   - Energy Potion as the first branch variety

Expected player moments:

- `Healing Potions pay the bills`
- `Mushrooms give me another reason to rearrange the room`
- `I can pick whether I push stable output or branch variety`

Priority unlocks:

- Mushroom Cave
- Energy Potion branch
- one or two machine-family upgrade lanes
- first room utility or premium placement area

The room should now feel:

- fuller
- busier
- more interesting
- close to layout tension but still readable

## 30 to 60 Minutes

Teach:

- demand should shape production priorities
- layout mastery matters more than buying random copies
- branch growth increases pressure in the starter room

Recommended sequence:

1. Demand shifts begin to matter:
   - stable healing requests
   - occasional premium Energy Potion ask
2. The player upgrades:
   - Mortar Golem speed or efficiency
   - Cauldron speed or quality
   - Bottling or shelf utility
3. The player encounters:
   - first obvious clutter tradeoff
   - first meaningful adjacency decision with visible consequence
4. The player earns:
   - first rare ingredient or special contract reward
   - first Arcane Study teaser if included in demo

Expected player moments:

- `I can solve this by changing layout, not just buying more stuff`
- `the room is starting to feel crowded in a good way`
- `I want one more branch, but I need better organization first`

Recommended progression targets by the 60-minute mark:

- stable Healing Potion loop
- live Mushroom branch
- visible demand handling
- at least 2 meaningful upgrades purchased
- at least 2 meaningful adjacency decisions made

## 60 to 120 Minutes

Teach:

- medium-term planning matters
- contracts and progression hooks can pull the pantry in different directions
- the next unlock should feel earned

Recommended sequence:

1. Introduce stronger demand pressure:
   - premium contracts
   - faction-like asks
   - timed opportunities with clear rewards
2. Unlock or tease:
   - first specialty utility machine
   - room expansion path
   - first proper prestige visibility if prestige is not yet reachable
3. The player improves:
   - room grouping
   - contract response
   - throughput consistency

Recommended reward types in this window:

- gold
- rare ingredient
- unlock progress
- prestige visibility
- Arcane Study seed resource if research is included

Expected player moments:

- `I’m not just scaling up, I’m shaping the pantry`
- `contracts are telling me what matters next`
- `I can see why I’ll want another room`

By the 2-hour mark, the player should have:

- one satisfying staple chain
- one branch chain
- clear contract-driven priorities
- meaningful spatial pressure
- clear anticipation for the next room, prestige, or research unlock

## 2 to 3 Hour Target

This window should bridge into one of two states:

### State A: Demo-Oriented Target

If the build is demo-scoped:

- the player approaches the first prestige teaser
- the player strongly feels second-room pressure
- the player has tasted one branching production path
- the player wants more room and more branch content

### State B: Full Early-Access-Oriented Target

If the build is beyond demo:

- the player unlocks the second room
- the player unlocks the first real Arcane Study options
- the player sees the first stronger faction progression hooks
- the player is on approach to first prestige

## Recommended Unlock Path Table

| Time Window | Primary Unlocks | Primary Upgrades | Main Player Question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 10 min | Mortar Golem, first demand card | first machine upgrade | how do I make one real potion line |
| 10 to 30 min | Mushroom Cave, Energy Potion branch | throughput upgrades | do I stabilize or branch |
| 30 to 60 min | premium placement, utility options, rare reward teaser | machine and room upgrades | how do I fix crowding and bottlenecks |
| 60 to 120 min | stronger contracts, room-growth pressure, prestige visibility | deeper chain tuning | what should this pantry become |
| 120 to 180 min | second-room unlock or strong preview, first Arcane Study if beyond demo | room specialization upgrades | what capability do I unlock next |

## Concrete Demo-Safe Unlock Path

If you want the cleanest demo path, use this exact unlock ladder:

1. Herb Garden
2. Well
3. Enchanted Cauldron
4. Mortar Golem
5. Healing Potion demand loop
6. first machine upgrade
7. Mushroom Cave
8. Energy Potion branch
9. first rare reward or faction teaser
10. second-room pressure teaser
11. prestige teaser

This is enough to prove:

- spatial readability
- visible automation
- branch growth
- demand-driven play
- medium-term aspiration

## Concrete Early Access Unlock Extension

Extend the demo path with:

1. first true second-room unlock
2. one source or prep specialty room
3. first Arcane Study track
4. first faction-progress-gated machine or recipe
5. prestige becoming fully reachable

## Upgrade Cadence Recommendations

The first 2 to 3 hours should not feel like pure unlock spam.

Use this rhythm:

- unlock
- stabilize
- upgrade
- branch
- reorganize
- reward
- tease next unlock

That rhythm fits the current product better than:

- unlock unlock unlock unlock unlock

or:

- wait 20 minutes for the next generic +10%

## Research Timing Recommendation

Research should not front-load the first 30 minutes.

Recommended timing:

- prototype: no research
- demo: maybe one teaser unlock
- Early Access: first proper Arcane Study track between hour 2 and hour 3

This keeps the early game focused on the pantry itself.

## Success Criteria For The First 2 to 3 Hours

The progression path is working if players can say:

- `I understand what my pantry is doing`
- `I know why production is blocked`
- `I made a meaningful layout improvement`
- `I unlocked a branch that changed my priorities`
- `I can see what I want next`

The progression path is failing if players mostly say:

- `I’m waiting`
- `I don’t know what to buy`
- `the room is cluttered but not interesting`
- `I unlocked things but do not care about them`
- `the next system just feels like another menu`
